Most people's first experience with generative AI is a chat window: you ask a question and get an answer. **Agentic AI** goes a step further. An AI agent doesn't just answer; it can plan a few steps, use tools, and complete a task.

## Chatbot vs. agent

A chatbot responds to a message. An agent is given a goal, such as "process this invoice" or "qualify this new lead", and can:

- read the relevant information (an email, a PDF, a CRM record),
- decide which steps are needed,
- use tools such as your CRM, spreadsheet, ticketing system, or database,
- and report back, or ask a person when it isn't sure.

The key difference is **action**. That is what makes agents useful, and also why they need careful design.

## Where agents help today

Agents work best on tasks that are frequent, follow a recognizable pattern, and still need some judgement:

- **Customer support:** classifying tickets, answering common questions from your documentation, and drafting replies for review.
- **Sales operations:** enriching and scoring new leads, updating the CRM, and preparing follow-up emails.
- **Document-heavy work:** extracting data from invoices, purchase orders, or contracts, and entering it into the right system.
- **Internal knowledge:** answering staff questions using your own policies and documents, with links to the source.

## Where to be careful

Agents are not magic, and pretending otherwise leads to failed projects. Be cautious when:

- **Mistakes are expensive or irreversible.** Payments, legal commitments, and medical decisions should keep a person in the loop.
- **The data is messy or missing.** An agent can only be as good as the information it can reach.
- **The process isn't defined.** If your team can't describe how a task should be done, an agent can't either.

## How to start safely

1. **Pick one workflow** that is high-volume and low-risk.
2. **Measure the current baseline:** time spent, error rate, and turnaround.
3. **Limit permissions.** Give the agent access only to what the task needs.
4. **Keep a human in the loop** for approvals and exceptions at first.
5. **Test with real examples** before going live, and keep monitoring after launch.
6. **Expand gradually** once results are proven.

## The bottom line

Agentic AI is most valuable when it is applied to a specific, well-understood business task, with clear limits and human oversight. Start small, measure honestly, and grow from there.
